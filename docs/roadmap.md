# Roadmap

[Product](product.md) · [Architecture](architecture.md)

This document is the source of truth for delivery order. Phase descriptions are outcomes and
candidate capabilities, not fixed architecture or launch dates.

## Phase 0: focused prototype

**Question:** Do people value discussing specific parts of a work, and does a contribution become
more useful when it appears in related contexts?

Start with 10–20 manually seeded works, work-level genomes, a small curated concept set, and one
evidence-backed debate. Support text and concept annotations, citations between addressable objects,
and projection of one contribution into related contexts. Preserve stable IDs, source provenance,
references across edits, and basic moderation. Use curated work and concept navigation; automated
ingestion, ontology maintenance, semantic search, recommendations, and full video analysis wait.

A prototype journey:

1. Open a curated film page and inspect its genome.
2. Read competing interpretations of a claim and inspect their sources.
3. Highlight a phrase or choose a concept, then add an annotation and cite an object.
4. Find that same contribution at its original target and through a related concept.
5. Follow a curated link to another work sharing the concept.

**Signals:** People annotate parts of works and arguments, rather than only writing general reviews;
people find contributions through related concepts after the original discussion has moved on. These
are hypotheses to test, not numerical launch thresholds.

## Phase 1: seeded network

**Question:** Do source-seeded pages remain useful before the community is large?

Candidate work: expand toward 100–500 culturally dense works and roughly 100–200 curated concepts;
add rights-aware source ingestion, source-derived claims with provenance, selected scenes, concept
pages, richer annotation targets, semantic search, and basic attribute-based recommendations.
Explore clustering without promoting an automated ontology yet. The corpus sizes are planning
estimates to revise after the prototype.

A later _The Shining_ page could connect its psychological-horror genome, the debate over the
Overlook's intent, the Steadicam tricycle sequence, and a claim about impossible architecture.
Source-derived topics would show expandable provenance, such as "Synthesized from 14 sources," only
when that count is real.

**Signal:** People explore and return to source-seeded pages with little native discussion.

## Phase 2: emergent ontology

**Question:** Can observed language yield useful concepts without an ever-growing manual taxonomy?

Candidate work: preserve observations, identify clusters and candidate relations, and introduce
reviewed promotion, merge, split, alias, and deprecation changes. Version changes and keep them
reversible. Evaluate concepts by whether they improve explanation or discovery.

## Phase 3: social knowledge depth

**Question:** Do citations and corrections help durable community knowledge form?

Candidate work: richer citations and annotation targets, domain-specific reputation, contribution
analytics, and the history of contested interpretations. Reward evidence and useful corrections
rather than raw popularity.

## Phase 4: discovery

**Question:** Does semantic discovery help people find works and ideas beyond title similarity?

Candidate work: concept-based exploration, personalized and explainable recommendations, affinity
signals, and semantic timelines of scenes, techniques, and discussion. Compare concept traversal and
genome-driven recommendations with simpler title-based discovery before expanding them.

## Phase 5: cultural intelligence

**Question:** Can aggregated interpretation data support a useful commercial product while
protecting individuals?

Candidate work: a semantic API, trend and research tools, audience intelligence, recommendation
infrastructure, and ontology licensing. Evaluate demand and rights before committing to a specific
offering. Keep individual behavior private; analyze aggregate cultural patterns.
